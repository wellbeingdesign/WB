Herbal Medicine: Nature’s Gift to Mankind

Long before modern science, herbal medicine was the foundation of health in nearly every civilization. From the indigenous peoples of the Americas to the wise healers of ancient Europe and Asia, plants have been used to treat ailments, strengthen the body, and promote longevity. These natural remedies contain compounds that our bodies recognize and can metabolize, working in harmony with our biological systems rather than against them.

Herbal medicines like castor oil, echinacea, ginger, turmeric, and valerian root have been used for centuries to boost immunity, reduce inflammation, and promote restful sleep. Unlike synthetic drugs, which often suppress symptoms and cause side effects, these herbs work to restore balance and support the body’s natural healing processes. This holistic approach doesn’t just treat symptoms — it seeks to eliminate the root cause of illness.

Essential Oils: Distilled Wisdom of the Earth

Essential oils are the concentrated essence of plants, distilled into powerful liquids that carry their healing properties. Used for both physical and emotional healing, oils like lavender, peppermint, and tea tree provide relief from ailments ranging from stress and headaches to skin conditions and infections. Their versatility makes them invaluable tools for those looking to live naturally and holistically.

Nicotine acts as a parasympathetic analogue to acetylcholine, targeting nicotinic receptors naturally present in the autonomic system. In low doses, it modulates blood pressure and autonomic tone within a clear therapeutic window—much like any other compound.

The ancient Knights Templar, known for their spiritual and physical prowess, were said to have used essential oils and herbal medicines in their healing rituals. They understood that the earth offers all that we need for health and vitality. The oils they used weren’t just tools — they were symbols of a higher connection to the natural world and its divine wisdom.

Sodium Bicarbonate: The Alchemical Elixir

Sodium bicarbonate, or baking soda, is another forgotten treasure in the world of natural healing. Far from being a mere kitchen ingredient, sodium bicarbonate holds the key to balancing the body’s pH and preventing the buildup of acidity — a root cause of many modern diseases. Acidity in the body has been linked to inflammation, cancer, and degenerative conditions. Yet, a simple, natural compound like baking soda can help neutralize acidity and promote overall health.

Important: Sodium bicarbonate is to be used for soaking in water containing it, not for ingestion.

        Blood Test Reference

        C-Reactive Protein (CRP): Measures general inflammation in the body.

        Neutrophil-to-Lymphocyte Ratio (NLR): Reflects the balance between innate and adaptive immune response and is used as a marker of systemic inflammation and stress.

        Interleukin-6 (IL-6): A cytokine that increases in response to infection and tissue injury.

        Tumor Necrosis Factor-Alpha (TNF-α): Involved in systemic inflammation and autoimmune conditions.

        White Blood Cell Count (WBC): Elevated levels indicate infection or immune response.

        Procalcitonin: High levels suggest bacterial infection rather than viral.

        Neutrophil Count (%): Indicates first-line immune response; high levels suggest bacterial infection or acute inflammation, while low levels may indicate viral influence or weakened immune response.

        Blood Culture: Used to detect bacterial or fungal infections in the bloodstream.

        D-Dimer: Elevated levels suggest blood clot formation.

        Activated Partial Thromboplastin Time (aPTT) (sec): Measures the efficiency of the intrinsic and common clotting pathways; prolonged aPTT suggests impaired clotting or anticoagulant effect, while shorter times may indicate increased clotting tendency.

        Prothrombin Time (PT) (sec): Measures how long it takes blood to clot; prolonged PT suggests impaired clotting (e.g., liver dysfunction, vitamin K deficiency, or anticoagulant effect), while shorter times may indicate increased clotting tendency.

        International Normalized Ratio (INR): Measures how quickly blood clots; high values indicate slower clotting and increased bleeding risk, while low values suggest faster clotting and potential risk of thrombosis.

        Fibrinogen: A clotting protein; increased in inflammation and cardiovascular risk.

        Platelet Count (x10⁹/L): Measures the number of platelets in the blood; low levels increase bleeding risk, while high levels may indicate inflammation or an increased risk of clot formation.

        Ancient Egyptian Foot Baths
        In Egypt, people used stone or pottery basins to soak their feet, often during rituals of cleansing and rejuvenation. These basins were sometimes adorned with symbolic carvings or motifs. Foot bathing served as a symbolic act of purification, frequently linked to spiritual or religious practices.

        Roman Foot Baths
        In ancient Rome, foot-soaking was common in bathhouses, where it prepared the body before full immersion. Romans viewed hygiene as essential, incorporating foot baths into elaborate rituals that included scrubbing, oiling, and massaging. Basins were made from marble, bronze, or stone—sometimes beautifully decorated.

        Chinese and Japanese Foot Soaking
        In traditional Chinese and Japanese medicine, soaking feet in hot water with herbs was believed to enhance vitality and longevity. Clay or metal basins were often used, with coals placed below or beside the vessel to maintain therapeutic warmth.

        Hindu Foot-Bathing Rituals
        In Hindu tradition, foot washing played a sacred role in hospitality and worship. Before entering temples or receiving honored guests, water-filled basins were used to wash the feet—offering both spiritual and symbolic respect.

        Biblical References to Foot Washing
        The Bible includes accounts of foot washing as a gesture of humility and service. While not always involving soaking bowls, basins filled with water were used in ceremonies that emphasized purification and reverence.

            Information entered without an account

            You can enter diagnostic information without registering. That information stays in the browser’s memory for the visit. It is not written to browser storage and it is not saved in the account database. If you register or sign in during the same visit, information still held in memory can be saved to the account.

            Account and sign-in

            Registration asks for a first name, surname, email address, and password. A telephone number is optional.

            The account record holds the email address, name, surname, a one-way password hash, the times the account was created and updated, a sign-in version, and an OSIRIS account id. The password itself is not stored. A telephone number you enter is kept in the profile.

            A signed-in session uses a token stored in the browser. It expires after seven days, unless a different lifetime has been set on the server. Signing out removes the token from that browser and invalidates existing tokens for the account. Resetting the password, and changing the login email address, also invalidate existing tokens. Signing out does not delete the account.

            Password-reset email is sent from osiris@wellbeingdesign.uk. The link expires after 60 minutes. OSIRIS stores a hash of the reset token.

            To limit repeated attempts, OSIRIS briefly stores the email address used, or the visitor’s network address, together with a count. This covers sign-in, registration, password reset, and a change of login email. The record lasts 15 minutes or one hour, depending on the action, and then it expires. It is not part of the medical record.

            Health information

            If you are signed in, the saved profile can include what you enter:

            • name, surname, email, and an optional telephone number
            • symptoms and questionnaire answers
            • age, sex, height, and weight
            • blood type and Rh factor
            • conditions and medications
            • laboratory values and vital signs
            • results from the OSIRIS analysis functions
            • ECG measurements
            • EQUIREVITAL records and timeline
            • imaging information saved into that profile

            Name and email are ordinary personal information. Symptoms, conditions, medicines, measurements, images, and analysis results are health information.

            ECG and radiology images

            Analysing an ECG image or a radiology image requires a signed-in account. Without a valid sign-in, the server refuses the upload and does not store an analysis record.

            When a model key is configured, the image and the analysis instruction are sent to the provider named below. The analysis record keeps the file name, file type, size, time, the extracted result, and a short copy of the model reply. It does not keep the image itself.

            If you are signed in, OSIRIS can also keep up to 20 ECG images and up to 20 radiology images in the EQUIREVITAL part of the profile. Those copies are stored with the profile in the database and in browser storage on a device where you are signed in.

            Doctors and GP email

            You can search for another OSIRIS account by email and request access. Nothing clinical is shared until that person accepts. You can cancel a pending request and withdraw access after it has been accepted.

            An accepted doctor can read the stored record: identity details, including a telephone number if it is in the profile; age, sex, height, weight, and blood type; questionnaire and diagnostic material; medications and conditions; EQUIREVITAL, including images archived there; and stored ECG and radiology analysis records. Messenger is not part of that record.

            “Ask GP” builds a message in your browser and opens your own email program, addressed to the GP email you type. OSIRIS does not send that email and does not keep a GP address book. The message includes patient details and suggested test names, and leaves out OSIRIS diagnosis narratives and AI interpretation fields.

            Messenger

            Messenger is for signed-in accounts. A search returns name, surname, and email. It does not return a medical record.

            Message text is stored for the conversation. Each person controls what they themselves can still see. Attachments — documents, images, or audio, up to 15 MB, and only in the types the server allows — are stored as files on the API server. A file can be downloaded only by a participant who can still see a message that refers to it. An old link does not override that rule. The link can contain the sign-in token. A token that has been signed out, reset, or replaced is refused.

            Every conversation starts on manual deletion. A 24-hour, 48-hour, or 7-day period applies only if you choose it for your own view. Your choice does not change the other person’s messages or files. An existing conversation stays on manual deletion until the person who wants a shorter period selects it.

            Remove contact takes that person off your list. Their account, their copy of the conversation, their files, and any medical record remain. You can add them again by search. A new message from them can bring the conversation back, including the earlier history.

            Delete conversation clears history for you only. Messages from before that moment stay hidden after a refresh or a later sign-in. The person remains in your list. The other person keeps their messages and files. A message sent after the clear appears in the usual way.

            A message and its file are removed from the server when every remaining participant’s own time limit has passed for that message. Until then, a person who can still see the message can still open the file. An upload that never becomes a message is removed after about one hour.

            OSIRIS keeps a short record when you remove a contact, clear your own copy of a conversation, change your retention setting, or when a new message restores a contact you had hidden. The record contains the account id, the action, the conversation id, the time, and the retention choice. It does not contain the message text or the file. These records are kept, and deleting the account does not delete them.

            If you delete your account, your own Messenger messages, the files you uploaded, and your own visibility settings are deleted. Messages and files sent by the other person remain available to them. Your name, email, and account id are removed from that conversation. OSIRIS does not put a substitute name in their place.
